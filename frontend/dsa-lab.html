💡 Why Head-Index Offset Matters ($O(1)$)

Standard array.shift() runs in $O(n)$ because it re-indexes every subsequent element. CampusPulse utilizes an internal head pointer index offset, achieving constant-time $O(1)$ dequeue operations without garbage collection overhead.

💡 Collision Resolution via Separate Chaining

When two distinct strings produce the same bucket index (collision), CampusPulse appends the node into a singly-linked chain at that bucket index. Average search time remains $O(1)$, degrading to $O(k)$ only where $k$ is chain length.

💡 Stability & Recurrence Relation

Merge Sort recurrence: $T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n)$. By Master Theorem, $T(n) \in \Theta(n \log n)$ in Best, Average, and Worst cases. Because left elements take precedence when equal (left[i] ≤ right[j]), it maintains absolute sorting stability.
